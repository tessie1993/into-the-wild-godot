# Collective Viability Gate — Operational Test

**Verdict:** NEEDS_WORK  ·  **Lane:** systems  ·  **Confidence:** medium  ·  **Platform:** differs

**Conclusion:** Define "viable path" as a bounded-turns reachability test — gap-to-threshold (read as a token position on physical tracks) compared against a printed "max possible gain in N rounds remaining" reference card. Failure denies the win attempt (game keeps running, bounded by the fixed turn budget) rather than granting catch-up help — which is also what defuses the "keep a rival barely alive" exploit, since suppressing a rival only delays the aggressor's own win, never rewards it.

---

## Observation

`canon/victory.json.collective_viability.viable_path_defined` is `false`. Spec §11/§13 states the rule ("a player can only win if every active player still has a viable path to win") but gives no test. At the table there is nothing to point at.

Two things make this tractable that the spec already provides:
1. **The game has a fixed turn budget.** `canon/modes.json`: standard = 15 turns/player. "Turns remaining" is a known, shrinking number, not open-ended.
2. **Every Duality/Light delta is already canon numbers**, not prose (`canon/duality.json.shifts`). So at least one axis of "how much could a trailing player possibly gain" is fully computable today, with no invented numbers.

The VP axis is *not* fully computable today — Give Back Light's VP reward is unspecified (GDD Q2) and no numeric VP thresholds exist yet (GDD Q6). I compute what canon supports and give the exact formula shape for the rest, rather than assert placeholder numbers as if they were derived.

## The mechanism: "Reach Card" gate

**Definition.** A player has a *viable path* for Way W if, for both of W's thresholds (VP and Light/Duality), their current gap to that threshold is ≤ the maximum anyone could possibly close in the rounds remaining — computed from the game's own best-case per-round deltas, not from that player's actual play skill. A player is viable overall if at least one of the 3 Ways clears.

**New/adapted physical components:**
- **Round tracker** — a numbered pip track or dial, 1 → turns_per_player (15 in standard mode). Needed regardless of Q7: it's also what makes the existing "exactly one more round" loop-preventer (spec §14) trackable. Propose formalizing it now.
- **VP track** — a printed pip track (mirrors the existing Duality track), so VP is a token position, not a written number. This is what turns "gap" into a *count of spaces* instead of mental subtraction of two possibly-large numbers.
- **Reach Card** — one printed reference card beside the 4D board:

| Rounds remaining | Max Light catchable | Max VP catchable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | *(OPEN — Q2/Q6)* |
| 2 | 6 | *(OPEN)* |
| 3 | 9 | *(OPEN)* |
| 4 | 12 | *(OPEN)* |
| 5 | 15 | *(OPEN)* |
| 6+ | 18, capped at 20 (full track span) | *(OPEN)* |

**Arithmetic behind the Light column (fully derived, not asserted):** Care phase and Action phase are separate per turn, and I6 only bounds the Action phase to 1-of-6 — so a player can stack Care's best Light gain with Action's best Light gain in the same round:
- Care phase: `gift_card` → **+1** Light (`canon/duality.json`)
- Action phase: `give_back_light` (id 6) or `guardian_giveback` via Quest (id 5) → **+2** Light (same file)
- Max Light/round = 1 + 2 = **3**. Over R rounds, ceiling = min(3R, 20) since the track is -10..+10.

**VP column formula (shape only, blocked on other open questions):** Quest and Give Back Light are the *same* Action-phase slot, so their VP is not additive — max VP/round = `max(hardest single quest's VP, Give Back Light's VP reward)`. Both operands are unspecified in canon today (Q2, Q6). Economy/systems lane fills this row once those land; the mechanism does not need to wait for that to be adopted.

## The check (procedure, ≤3 look-ups, no arithmetic)

Runs whenever, after the 4D board exists, any active player's stats reach a Way's thresholds:
1. Read the round tracker → rounds remaining, R.
2. For each *other* active player: for each of the 3 Ways, count the physical gap between their token and that Way's threshold peg on the VP track and the Duality track. Look up row R on the Reach Card. If both gaps (VP and Light) for at least one Way are ≤ that row's numbers, the player is viable.
3. If every other active player is viable on at least one Way → the proposed winner's victory stands (still subject to the existing loop-preventer, spec §14).
4. If any player clears on zero Ways → victory is **denied for now**. Play continues normally — no artificial round is added, no catch-up bonus is granted (see below for why not). The check re-runs the next time any player crosses a threshold, or at the final round from Master Guardian defeat / loop-preventer.
5. **Hard backstop:** because turns_per_player is fixed, this cannot stall indefinitely. If the round tracker hits the mode's final round with no confirmed winner, resolve with the *existing* tiebreak ladder already in `canon/victory.json` (higher Light → more Guardian-quest VP → shared victory), applied to standings at that moment. No new numbers needed here — just wiring the fallback path to an existing rule.

## Failure handling — why "denied, not helped"

Considered three options: winner-doesn't-win-but-nothing-else-happens (risks stalling), extend-the-game (risks the exact degenerate case flagged), or inject-a-catch-up-bonus-for-the-trailing-player (risks a *different* one — free VP for staying last). Chose **denial that self-resolves via the fixed turn cap**:
- No stall: the round tracker is finite (mode-defined), so "keeps not-winning" always terminates at the existing tiebreak fallback.
- No new number to argue about: the fallback reuses `canon/victory.json.tiebreakers`, already canon.

## Pressure test: "keep a rival barely alive but permanently last"

The failure mode denies the *aggressor's own* victory — it grants the trailing player nothing. That flips the incentive the concern is worried about:
- There is no reward for precisely engineering a rival's gap to sit exactly at the reach-ceiling boundary. The ceiling is a **generous, best-single-action ceiling** (loosest defensible number derived honestly from canon deltas), so it's cheap to stay clear of without deliberate management — the rule is a rare-case backstop against true runaway blowouts, not a constant handicap tax.
- The game is single-winner and ends at the first confirmed victory (or the hard turn cap) — there is no repeatable loop where suppressing a rival pays off turn after turn. Suppressing a rival only *delays* the aggressor's own win, which is self-defeating, not profitable.
- Residual risk, not eliminated: a *third* player could push a rival past the ceiling purely to deny the leader (spite/kingmaker), not to win themselves. Bounded by the same fixed-turn-cap fallback — worst case the game runs its full course and resolves by existing tiebreak — but this is a known multiplayer pathology, flagged as an open risk rather than solved here (would need input from whoever owns spec §13's amplified-setbacks interaction).

## Dependencies before this becomes fully operational

- **Q2** (Give Back Light VP reward) and **Q6** (numeric VP/Light thresholds) must land before the Reach Card's VP column and the threshold pegs themselves can be printed with real numbers.
- **VP track** as a physical component is a prerequisite for the "count spaces, no arithmetic" property; if VP stays a written scorepad number, this degrades gracefully to mental subtraction (still fast, but no longer literally "seconds, zero arithmetic").

## Adversarial review

**Verdict:** NEEDS_WORK

**Math error found:** light_reach_per_round=3 is derived solely from canon/duality.json (main-board deltas: gift_card +1, give_back_light/guardian_giveback +2), but the gate it feeds applies only "when 4D Board is built" (victory.json), where spec §13 states board rewards are doubled-or-more (2x+) and setbacks doubled — a multiplier the proposal never applies, so the real ceiling for the governing context is at least ~6, not 3. Separately, the Care-phase +1 assumes one trade per Care phase, a cap that appears nowhere in spec/economy-engine/actions-v1-draft; with no stated limit, an N-player game (N>2, which this design supports) allows up to N-1 gift-trades in one Care phase, pushing the true Care-phase ceiling above 1.

**Pillars blocked:** Balance — the printed light_reach_per_round=3 ceiling is computed from the main-board canon/duality.json delta table, but the gate only applies when the 4D Board is built (victory.json collective_viability.applies_when), where spec §13 states rewards are 'doubled or more (2x+)' and setbacks are 'doubled.' An undercounted ceiling reopens the exact lock-a-rival-out-on-paper exploit the mechanism claims to defuse, since the true achievable catch-up rate on the governing board is higher than what's printed., Depth/Balance — Way 3 (Dark path) is never derived: the Reach Card only computes the positive-Light direction, and spec §13's 'Dark Path Exclusion: players on the Dark path cannot enter or do anything on the 4D board' is never addressed, despite bearing directly on whether/how Way 3 factors into a gate scoped to the 4D board.

**Physical problem:** "≤3 look-ups, no arithmetic" undersells the actual table procedure once player count exceeds 2: confirming a lockout requires counting token-to-peg gaps across 2 axes (VP, Light) × 3 Ways for each of up to N-1 other active players, and once Q6 lands the tracks need up to 6 distinct threshold pegs (3 Ways × 2 axes) placed and correctly identified per player — not fatal since the check is rare (fires only on threshold-crossing), but the "seconds, zero arithmetic" framing is optimistic beyond a 2-player game.


The one number this proposal claims to have "fully derived, not asserted" — light_reach_per_round = 3 — does not survive a check against the very rule that defines when the gate fires.

1. **Wrong ruleset for the phase it governs (the decisive error).** `canon/victory.json.collective_viability.applies_when` is explicitly "4D Board is built." But spec §13 states, for the 4D board specifically: "Rewards... are doubled or more (2x+) compared to the main board" and "Negative aspects, penalties, and setbacks are doubled on this board." The proposal derives its +1 (Care, gift_card) and +2 (Action, give_back_light/guardian_giveback) entirely from `canon/duality.json.shifts`, whose `$source` is "spec §5, §6; economy-engine.md §4.3" — the main-board table. It never applies the 4D board's 2x+ multiplier. If the same triggers pay double-or-more on the board where this gate actually operates, light_reach_per_round is at least 6, not 3 — the proposal's headline "fully computed number" is very likely wrong by 2x or more, for the exact context it was built to cover. This isn't a minor rounding gap: it's the single number the proposal set out to prove was derivable, computed from the wrong board's economy.

2. **Silent, unstated cap on Care-phase trades.** The Care-phase ceiling of +1 assumes exactly one trade per Care phase. Nothing in `into-the-wild-spec.md`, `economy-engine.md` §4.3, or `actions/actions-v1-draft.md` caps trades-per-Care-phase — the "trading window" language never says "one trade." In an N-player game (the game explicitly supports more than 2 — salvage draft scales "2 x player count," co-op mode mentions 2v2 pairs), a resource-rich player could in principle execute a `gift_card`/`generous_trade_3ce` trigger with each of the N-1 other players in a single Care phase, for up to (N-1) Light from Care alone, not 1. Note also that spec's actual source text for this trigger — "Gifting a card (**or** trading with a net value disparity of ≥3 CE in their favor) instantly awards the giver +1 Light" (`actions-v1-draft.md` line 13) — reads as one OR-gated award per trade event, which canon then split into two same-delta JSON rows; the proposal correctly avoided double-counting *within* one trade, but never addressed counting *across* multiple trades with multiple partners.

Both errors point the same direction: the printed ceiling likely **undercounts** the true achievable catch-up rate. That directly undermines the proposal's own "Pressure test" section, which depends on the ceiling being a genuinely *generous* best-case number that "rarely triggers except in genuinely extreme late-game blowouts." An undercounted ceiling is a *tight* one — it would deny victories in cases where the trailing player's true (legal, on-paper-achievable) catch-up rate is actually higher than the card says, reopening exactly the "engineer a rival's gap to sit just past the printed boundary" exploit the mechanism was built to close, except now the boundary itself is wrong rather than the players' intent.

3. **Way 3 (Dark) is never actually derived, and a directly relevant rule is never engaged.** The Reach Card only computes the positive-Light direction (toward Enlightened/Capable thresholds). Way 3's threshold is `max_dark` — the opposite direction on the same track — and the proposal never derives (or even flags needing) a negative-direction ceiling. Worse, spec §13 states "**Dark Path Exclusion:** Players on the Dark path (negative Duality points) cannot enter or do anything on the 4D board" — meaning Dark-path players are barred from the very board where `collective_viability.applies_when` says this gate operates. The proposal's "viable if ≥1 of 3 Ways clears" logic never engages with this exclusion, which bears directly on whether Way 3 needs checking at all under this gate, or how an excluded player can even be evaluated against a Reach Card "beside the 4D board."

4. **Minor — premature flag flip.** `viable_path_defined` is set to `true` while `vp_reach_per_round` is left `OPEN`. Since the check requires *both* the VP gap and the Light gap to clear for a Way, the gate cannot actually resolve for any Way today — the mechanism isn't operational yet, only specified. Calling the flag `true` overstates what's usable at the table right now; it would be more honest to leave it `false` (or a distinct "partially specified" state) until Q2/Q6 land.

5. **Minor — physical-load claim oversold.** "≤3 look-ups, no arithmetic" describes reading the Reach Card row, but the actual worst-case procedure is counting token-to-peg gaps across 2 axes × 3 Ways × up to (N-1) other players before a failure is confirmed, plus needing up to 6 distinct threshold pegs marked on two tracks once Q6 lands. This is likely still fast at the table (the check only fires when someone crosses a threshold, so it's rare), but it's not the "seconds, zero arithmetic" framing implies for anything beyond 2 players.

No formal invariant (I1–I15) is broken, and `pytest`/`python -m sim` both pass clean against current canon since nothing in this proposal is wired into the test suite yet — but that's exactly the problem: the proposal's central number was never checked against the board-specific modifier (§13) that governs the phase it claims to gate, and that omission is large enough (2x+) to invalidate the "fully derived, not asserted" claim it rests on.


**Strongest part:** The failure-handling design is genuinely good: "deny, don't help" self-terminates on the fixed turns_per_player budget (canon/modes.json) and falls back to the *existing* victory.json.tiebreakers ladder rather than inventing new numbers. The pressure-test reasoning that suppressing a rival only delays the aggressor's own win (never rewards it, since the game is single-winner and ends at first confirmed victory or the hard cap) is structurally sound regardless of the ceiling's exact value. Correctly relying on I6 to treat Quest (id5, guardian_giveback +2) and Give Back Light (id6, +2) as mutually exclusive rather than additive is the one piece of arithmetic in the proposal that is actually airtight against canon.
